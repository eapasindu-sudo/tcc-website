About us

A home of learning for Thelijjawila

Thelijjawila Central College is a mixed national school for boys and girls in Thelijjawila, Matara, in the Southern Province. It stands on a hillock at Udukawa facing the paddy fields, close to the Weligama–Akuressa main road. More than 2,500 students in Grades 6 to 13 learn here in Sinhala and English, guided by 130 teachers.

The college was founded in 1946 as a Buddhist school under the education-for-villages scheme of Dr. C. W. W. Kannangara, the Father of Free Education, with the support of David Wanigasekara and Ven. Naranda Thero of Wajiraramaya, Bambalapitiya. It was declared a national school on 31 January 1996.

Our crest carries the pun kalasaya, the Dharma Chakra, the oil lamp and the open books, which stand for prosperity, wisdom and the light of education that our motto speaks of. The school colours are maroon and gold.

Vision

To be a centre of excellence that produces disciplined, knowledgeable and caring citizens for Sri Lanka.

Mission

To give every child a quality education, develop their talents and values, and prepare them to face the future with confidence.

Our history

From a thatched hut to a national school

  1. 17 Jan 1946

    Classes begin at Telijjawila junction in a small coconut-thatched building, with nearly 200 students and three teachers. The pioneer teachers were D. H. Wickramasekara and U. A. Podinona, and the first principal was H. L. Wimalasuriya from Gnanodaya Maha Vidyalaya, Kalutara.

  2. Early years

    Wickramaratne Walawwa serves as the boys' hostel and Samarasinghe Walawwa in Bibugodella watta as the girls' hostel. Lessons are taught in English until 1956.

  3. 1948 – 1955

    The school moves to its present home on the hillock at Udukawa, with an assembly hall, classrooms, administrative blocks, craft rooms and two hostel blocks.

  4. 15 Jul 1955

    Prime Minister Sir John Kotelawala formally declares the school open, during the time of principal R. B. Gallella.

  5. 31 Jan 1996

    Thelijjawila Central College is declared a national school.

  6. Today

    Over 2,500 students and 130 teachers, with four houses: Rohana, Thissa, Anura and Vijitha.
